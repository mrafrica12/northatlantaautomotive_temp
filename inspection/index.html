You see the problem before you pay for it

The short answer

Our technician photographs and films what they find and sends it to your phone. You see the worn brake pad or the leaking gasket yourself, with a price beside each item, and you approve or decline line by line — before any work starts.

How it works

  1. You drop the car off. Tell us what it's doing and when it started.
  2. We inspect and document. The technician photographs everything they find, and films it where movement matters — a wobbling wheel bearing or an active drip is far clearer in video.
  3. You get a text. A link opens the inspection on your phone: each finding, its photograph, and what it costs to put right.
  4. You decide, item by item. Approve what you want done, decline the rest. We only do what you approved.

You can decline anything

This is worth saying plainly, because it's the part people don't quite believe. Every item is separate. Approving the brakes and declining the air filter is completely normal. Nothing gets added to the bill without you saying yes to it first.

If something is a genuine safety issue we'll tell you clearly and explain why. But the decision stays yours, and we'd rather you drove away having declined something than felt cornered into it.

What it doesn't do

A digital inspection doesn't make a shop honest by itself. Photographs can still be presented misleadingly, and plenty of shops that use this software still work on commission.

What it does is make dishonesty checkable. You can take those photographs to another shop and ask what they think. We're comfortable with that — it's the point.

Why this matters more than anything else on this website

The oldest problem in this trade is that you can't see what the technician sees. Everything people believe about repair shops flows from that gap. Closing it is the single most useful thing we do, and until now it was invisible to anyone who hadn't already brought us a car.

Do I have to be there in person?

No. It arrives as a link on your phone. Approving it from your desk at work is the normal way people use it.

Does it cost extra?

No. It's simply how we inspect.

Can I get a copy afterwards?

Yes, and it's worth keeping — a documented service history is a real asset when you come to sell the car.

What if I don't understand what I'm looking at?

Call us and we'll walk you through it. That's a completely reasonable thing to ask, and a shop that's impatient about it is telling you something.
