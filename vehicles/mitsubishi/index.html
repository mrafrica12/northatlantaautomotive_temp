The short version

What is actually different about servicing a Mitsubishi

Most modern Mitsubishis use a continuously variable transmission, and it is the single most important thing to get right. A CVT does not shift between fixed gears — it squeezes a steel belt between variable pulleys, and the fluid is what allows that belt to grip instead of slip. The friction characteristics are engineered as part of the transmission, which is why Mitsubishi specifies their own fluid rather than a generic CVT product. A universal fluid can behave acceptably for a while and then produce shudder and belt wear that no later fluid change will reverse.

The all-wheel-drive systems — Mitsubishi's AWC and the more sophisticated S-AWC — add a transfer case and a rear differential, each with their own fluid and their own interval. Nothing on the dashboard mentions them, so they are among the most commonly skipped services on the brand. The symptom of neglect is a bind or a groan in tight low-speed turns.

The Outlander PHEV is a different vehicle again: a high-voltage battery, its own cooling arrangements, and a petrol engine that runs less often than any engine was designed to. Call us with your model and year and we will tell you exactly which parts of it we handle.

Pattern recognition

What we see most on Mitsubishis

These are the specific things worth knowing about your make — not a generic checklist. If none of them describe your car, we will tell you that too.

CVT fluid is brand-specific

Mitsubishi specifies its own CVT fluid because the transmission is designed around that fluid's friction properties. Generic fluid causes shudder and belt wear, and by the time it shows the damage is done.

AWC and S-AWC driveline fluids

The transfer case and rear differential have separate fluids with separate intervals and no dashboard reminder. Binding or groaning in tight turns is the usual first symptom.

MIVEC and cold-start rattle

A brief rattle on start-up on higher-mileage MIVEC engines usually points at timing chain tension or oil pressure at the actuator. Worth investigating early rather than living with.

PHEV high-voltage systems

The Outlander PHEV has a traction battery, its own thermal management and an engine that runs infrequently. Tell us the model and year and we will tell you what we can and cannot take on.

Mitsubishi questions

Common questions from Mitsubishi owners

Why does Mitsubishi insist on its own CVT fluid?

Because the fluid is part of the transmission's design, not a consumable that happens to go in it. The belt grips the pulleys through the fluid, and that grip depends on friction properties engineered specifically for the unit. A generic fluid can seem fine for months and then produce shudder and belt wear that a later fluid change will not undo.

My Outlander shudders gently when accelerating from a stop. Is the transmission failing?

Not necessarily, but it is worth looking at now rather than later. On a CVT that shudder is often the first indication that the fluid is past its useful life. Caught at that point it is frequently a fluid service. Left alone it usually is not.

How often should the CVT fluid be changed?

Sooner than the maximum interval implies for most of our customers, because Atlanta traffic is harder on a CVT than steady highway driving. We check the condition and tell you where you actually are rather than quoting from a chart.

Do you service the Outlander PHEV?

For conventional maintenance, brakes, suspension, cooling and diagnostics, generally yes. High-voltage battery work is a different scope. Call us with your year and model and we will give you a straight answer before you drive over.
