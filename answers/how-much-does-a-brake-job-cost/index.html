How Much Does a Brake Job Cost?

The short answer

A front brake job — pads and rotors on one axle — is the most common brake repair. What moves the price is your vehicle, the parts grade, and whether the calipers are still healthy. We publish our ranges rather than making you call for a number, and we photograph the measurements so you can see why.

What actually drives the price

Vehicle first — a European car with larger brakes and electronic parking brake actuators costs more than a common sedan. Parts grade second. Third, condition: a seized caliper or a sheared bleeder adds work that was not visible when you booked. We will show you the photograph before that becomes a bill.

Front and rear are not the same price

Fronts do most of the braking and wear roughly twice as fast, so they come up more often. Rears on vehicles with an electronic parking brake need a scan tool to retract the caliper piston, which adds a step. Doing all four when only the fronts are worn is the single most common piece of unnecessary brake spending.

Pads only, or pads and rotors?

If the rotors are above the manufacturer's minimum thickness and running true, pads alone is the honest answer and it is meaningfully cheaper. We measure and show you rather than defaulting to the bigger job.

Why we publish this at all

Our technicians are salaried rather than commissioned, so nobody here earns more by selling you a larger job. Publishing the range is how we make that checkable instead of just claiming it.

Related questions

Why is the dealer quote so much higher?

Usually higher labour rates and OE-only parts. Sometimes the dealer is right — some vehicles genuinely want OE brakes. We will tell you which case you are in.

Do you price match?

We would rather show you what we are fitting and why. A cheaper quote using lower-grade pads on rotors that should have been replaced is not the same job.
