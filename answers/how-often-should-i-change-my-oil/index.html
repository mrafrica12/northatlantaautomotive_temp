How Often Should You Change Your Oil?

The short answer

Most modern engines running full synthetic go somewhere between 7,500 and 10,000 miles between changes. The 3,000-mile rule is a holdover from conventional oil and older engines. Your owner's manual or dashboard maintenance minder has the real number — and following it is not cutting corners.

Where to find your actual interval

The owner's manual, or the maintenance minder on the dash if your car has one. Manufacturers spend a lot of money determining this figure, and modern oils genuinely last longer than the ones the 3,000-mile habit was built around.

What counts as severe service

Lots of short trips where the engine never fully warms. Sitting in heavy traffic. Towing. Sustained high temperatures — which describes an Atlanta summer. If most of your driving looks like that, use the shorter severe-service interval in the manual. This is a real distinction, not a sales tactic.

Time matters as well as mileage

Oil degrades in the sump even if you barely drive. If you cover very few miles, change it annually regardless of the odometer.

Why we do not put a 3,000-mile sticker on your windscreen

Because for most cars on this road it would be wrong, and it would be wrong in the direction that makes us money. If your manufacturer says 7,500, we will tell you 7,500.

Related questions

Is synthetic worth it if my car doesn't require it?

It handles heat better and lasts longer, so it often works out similar per mile. If your manufacturer requires it, it is not optional.

Does an oil change include anything else?

Ours includes a genuine multi-point inspection where anything flagged is photographed and sent to you. Nothing is added to the bill without your approval.
