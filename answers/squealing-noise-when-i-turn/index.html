Squealing Noise When You Turn

The short answer

A squeal that appears when you turn the wheel, especially at low speed or parking, usually means a worn or slipping accessory belt loading up as the power steering pump works harder. A groan or whine rather than a squeal points more at the power steering fluid or the pump itself.

Is it safe to drive?

Generally yes in the short term, though a belt that is slipping badly can fail entirely — and on many engines that same belt drives the water pump and alternator, so a snapped belt means overheating and no charging at once.

Squeal versus groan

A high-pitched squeal that rises with engine speed is usually the belt. A low groan or whine that follows the steering rather than the engine is more likely the power steering pump or low fluid. Describing which one you hear genuinely narrows the diagnosis.

Worse when cold, better when warm

That is classic belt behaviour. A glazed or perished belt slips most when cold and stiff, then grips better as it warms. It usually means the belt is due rather than the tensioner having failed outright.

Clicking rather than squealing

A rhythmic click that speeds up with the car and only appears on tight turns is usually a CV joint on a front-wheel-drive car — a different repair entirely, and one that gets worse steadily.

Related questions

Can I use belt dressing spray?

It quietens the noise briefly and makes the underlying wear harder to assess. We would rather replace a worn belt than mask it.

Is electric power steering different?

Yes — many newer cars have no power steering belt or pump at all, so a squeal on those points elsewhere.
