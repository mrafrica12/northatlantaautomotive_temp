Transmission Fluid Change vs Flush

The short answer

A drain and fill replaces roughly a third to a half of the fluid. A flush exchanges nearly all of it using a machine. On a well-maintained transmission either is fine. On a high-mileage one that has never been serviced, many technicians — including us — prefer the gentler drain and fill.

What each one actually does

A drain and fill empties the pan and refills it. Much of the fluid stays in the torque converter and cooler lines, so you replace part of it — repeating it a few thousand miles later gets you most of the way there gradually. A flush connects a machine to the cooler lines and exchanges nearly everything in one go.

The honest position on flushing neglected transmissions

There is genuine debate here and we will not pretend otherwise. The concern is that a transmission with a lot of miles on old fluid has worn in a particular way, and a full exchange changes its behaviour suddenly. Some technicians consider that risk overstated. We tend toward the conservative option and will explain our reasoning for your specific vehicle rather than applying one rule to everything.

If it is already slipping, neither will fix it

Fresh fluid does not repair worn clutch material. If the transmission is slipping under load, flaring between gears, or shifting harshly, that needs diagnosis. Anyone selling you a fluid service as the cure for a slipping transmission is selling you something.

Fluid specification is not negotiable

Modern transmissions call for fluid meeting an exact manufacturer standard, and the wrong fluid causes shudder, poor shifting or damage. Universal fluids that claim to cover everything are a false economy.

Related questions

My manufacturer says lifetime fluid — is that real?

It means designed to last the transmission's design life, which may be shorter than you want the car to last. Many technicians recommend servicing anyway, particularly with towing or heavy traffic.

How often should it be done?

Commonly every 30,000 to 60,000 miles where the manufacturer specifies an interval. Check your manual, and mention it if you tow.
