Dealership or Independent Mechanic?

The short answer

Independents are usually cheaper on labour and more flexible on parts. Dealerships are the right call for warranty work, open recalls, and faults needing manufacturer-specific software or newly released technical bulletins. For routine maintenance and most repairs on a car past warranty, a good independent generally wins on price and on continuity of who actually touches your car.

When the dealership is genuinely the better answer

If your car is under factory warranty, go to the dealer — you have already paid for that work. The same applies to open recalls, which are free at a dealer and cannot be done anywhere else. Some faults also need proprietary software or a very recent technical bulletin that only the franchise network has. We will tell you when you are in one of those cases, and send you there.

Where an independent usually wins

Labour rates are typically lower. There is more flexibility on parts grade, so you can choose where to spend. And you often deal with the same people over years — at a dealership, service advisors and technicians change frequently, and nobody accumulates knowledge of your particular car.

The myth worth killing

Using an independent shop does not void your factory warranty. Under US federal law a manufacturer cannot require you to use dealer servicing to keep a warranty valid, provided the work is done correctly and to specification and you keep records. Keep your invoices, and you are fine.

The question that actually matters

Not dealer versus independent, but whether whoever is working on your car shows you evidence. A shop that photographs the worn part and lets you decline any line item is behaving well regardless of the sign outside. That is the standard worth applying to us too.

Related questions

Will using an independent void my warranty?

No. Federal law protects your right to have maintenance done elsewhere without losing warranty coverage, as long as it is done properly and documented.

Can independents access manufacturer information?

Largely yes — service information and most diagnostic capability is available to independents, though some programming remains restricted.

Are dealership parts better?

OE parts are matched to the vehicle, and for some components that matters. For many others, quality aftermarket parts are equivalent or better. It depends on the part.
