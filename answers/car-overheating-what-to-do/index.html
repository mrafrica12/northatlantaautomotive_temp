Your Car Is Overheating — What to Do Now

The short answer

Pull over safely and switch the engine off. Continuing to drive an overheating engine can warp a cylinder head or destroy a head gasket in a few miles, turning a modest repair into a very large one. Do not open the radiator cap while it is hot — the system is pressurised and the coolant is above boiling point.

Is it safe to drive?

No. This is the one symptom on this site where the honest answer is stop driving immediately. The difference between pulling over now and pushing on for another ten miles is frequently the difference between a hose and an engine.

What to do in the first five minutes

Turn the AC off and the heater on full — the heater core is a small radiator and will pull some heat out of the engine. Pull over somewhere safe and switch off. Do not open the radiator cap or the expansion tank while hot. Wait at least thirty minutes before looking. If there is steam or a sweet smell, you have lost coolant.

The usual causes

A split or perished hose. A leaking radiator, often at the plastic end tanks. A failed water pump, sometimes with bearing noise beforehand. A thermostat stuck shut. A failed cooling fan, which produces the distinctive pattern of overheating in traffic but running normally at speed. A blown head gasket, which is both a cause and a consequence.

Overheats in traffic, fine on the highway

That pattern points strongly at airflow rather than coolant flow — usually the cooling fan. At speed, air is forced through the radiator regardless; in traffic, only the fan moves it. It is a genuinely useful thing to notice and mention.

Why we check for head gasket damage

If the engine has already been run hot, we test for combustion gases in the coolant before assuming a simple repair will hold. Replacing a hose on an engine that has already cooked its gasket would just hand you a bigger bill a month later.

Related questions

Can I just add water and keep driving?

Water will get you off the roadside in an emergency, but it does not have the boiling point, freezing point or corrosion protection of proper coolant. Treat it as a get-home measure only.

Why is my coolant disappearing with no visible leak?

Either a very slow leak that evaporates before it drips, or coolant being burnt in the combustion chamber — which points at a head gasket.
