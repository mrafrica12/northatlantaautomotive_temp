Check Engine Light Diagnostics

What to expect

A check engine light stores a code, but the code names a circuit or symptom — not the broken part. Code P0420 does not mean you need a catalytic converter. Proper diagnosis means testing the circuit to find which component actually failed. In Gwinnett County this matters twice over: a lit check engine light is an automatic emissions test failure.

Why a free code read isn't a diagnosis

The parts store scan tells you which monitor set the code. That is the starting point, not the answer. P0171 (system too lean) can be a vacuum leak, a dirty mass airflow sensor, a weak fuel pump, or a leaking injector — four different repairs at four very different prices. Replacing parts based on the code alone is guessing with your money.

Is it safe to drive?

A steady light usually means drive it, but get it looked at soon. A flashing light means stop driving and get it towed — flashing indicates a misfire severe enough to dump raw fuel into the catalytic converter, which can destroy a very expensive part in a short distance. If the light is flashing, do not finish your errands first.

What we actually do

Pull all codes and freeze-frame data. Check for manufacturer service bulletins. Test the actual circuit — live data, wiring, and the component itself. Photograph what we find and send it to you with a written explanation and a price before any repair is approved.

Common questions

Will my car pass emissions with the light on?

No. Under Georgia's Clean Air Force programme a lit check engine light is an automatic failure, regardless of what the tailpipe reads.

The light went off by itself. Am I fine?

The fault is still stored, and the readiness monitors will have reset. If you are due for emissions testing, a car with recently cleared codes will fail on incomplete monitors even though the light is off.
