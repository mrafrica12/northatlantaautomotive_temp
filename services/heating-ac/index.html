Car Air Conditioning Repair

What to expect

If your A/C is blowing warm, the usual cause is low refrigerant — and refrigerant only gets low because it leaked. We find the leak with dye or an electronic detector rather than just topping it up, because a recharge without a repair is money that leaks back out over a few weeks.

Why we won't just recharge it

A sealed system does not consume refrigerant. If it is low, it escaped somewhere — a hose, an O-ring, the condenser, or the compressor shaft seal. Topping it up without fixing that gets you a few weeks of cold air and a repeat bill, and it vents refrigerant into the atmosphere. We would rather find the leak.

R-134a or R-1234yf?

Vehicles built from roughly 2015 onward increasingly use R-1234yf, which costs several times more than the older R-134a and needs different equipment. This is the single biggest variable in an A/C bill, and it is set by what your car came with — we will tell you which you have before quoting.

It's cold at speed but warm at idle

That usually points at airflow across the condenser rather than the refrigerant charge — often a failed cooling fan or a condenser packed with road debris. It is a genuinely different repair from a leak, and usually cheaper.

Common questions

How long does A/C diagnosis take?

Usually about an hour to test pressures and find the leak. Dye tests sometimes need you to drive the car for a few days and come back.

Is a weak A/C worth fixing on an older car?

Sometimes not, and we will say so. If the compressor has failed and contaminated the system on a car worth a few thousand dollars, that is a conversation worth having honestly.
