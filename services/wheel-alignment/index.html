Wheel Alignment

What to expect

If your car pulls to one side, the steering wheel sits off-centre, or your tyres are wearing unevenly on one edge, alignment is the usual cause. Potholes and kerb strikes are the usual trigger. An alignment protects a set of tyres worth far more than the alignment itself.

How to tell you need one

The steering wheel is not straight when the car is going straight. The car drifts toward one side on a level road. The inside or outside edge of a front tyre is visibly more worn than the rest of the tread. Any of those three is worth checking.

Alignment is not the same as balancing

Alignment sets the angles the wheels sit at. Balancing corrects weight distribution within a wheel and tyre. Alignment fixes pulling and uneven wear; balancing fixes vibration at speed. They are different jobs and they solve different symptoms.

Some vehicles need more than an alignment

If your car has forward-facing cameras or radar for lane keeping or adaptive cruise, changing the alignment can require those systems to be recalibrated afterwards. We will tell you if yours is one of them.

Common questions

How often should I get an alignment?

There is no fixed interval. Get one when you notice a symptom, after hitting a serious pothole, and when fitting new tyres.
